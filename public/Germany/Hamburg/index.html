Hamburg: model railways, the harbour and breakfast on the Alster

1 October 2019Germany, HamburgVisited Jul 2022, Oct 2019, Jul 2018, Jul 2016, Apr 2011

Key takeaways

  • Go for: a big harbour city with world-class attractions, grand architecture and lakeside cafés
  • Don't miss: Miniatur Wunderland, the largest model railway in the world
  • Best coffee: not scored yet
  • Skip: nothing in particular
  • Pro tip: start the day with breakfast at ALEX, overlooking the Alster lake in the middle of town

The brief

Five trips between 2011 and 2022, visiting friends who live in Hamburg and staying with them.

Top placements

1. Miniatur Wunderland


The largest model railway and miniature airport in the world. Over 16 km of track runs through sections including the Alps, Scandinavia, America, Italy, a replica of Hamburg Airport and Hamburg itself, with around 1,300 trains, 130,000 trees and 400,000 tiny figures. Website · Map

2. A harbour boat tour

Hamburg is Europe's third-largest port, after Rotterdam and Antwerp, and a boat tour round the docks is the best way to see the scale of it.

3. Hamburg City Hall


The Rathaus, built between 1886 and 1897 after the old city hall burned down in the Great Fire of 1842. It's the seat of Hamburg's state parliament. Website · Map

  • Tip: it officially has 647 rooms, but in 1971 a forgotten room in the tower was found by accident while someone was hunting for a document that had fallen behind a filing cabinet. There may be more.

4. St. Nikolai Memorial


The ruins of a church first built in 1195 and dedicated to Saint Nicholas, patron saint of sailors. The rebuilt church, designed by the English architect George Gilbert Scott, was the tallest building in the world from 1874 to 1876. A lift takes you up to a 75 m viewing platform. Website · Map

5. Breakfast at ALEX


A brasserie in the Alsterpavillon, styled on French coffee houses, looking straight out over the Alster lake. The best breakfast spot in town. Website · Map

Ad spotting

Plenty of high-impact out-of-home sites, and the metro has lots of placements, including D6s. Hamburg has a lot of people with high disposable income, so it's a good market for fashion and high-ticket items.

Good to know

  • Hamburg's full name is the Free and Hanseatic City of Hamburg. It's Germany's second-largest city and one of its 16 federal states.
  • The name comes from Hammaburg, a castle built on the site in the 9th century.
  • After the Great Fire of 1842, it took almost 44 years to build the new City Hall, at a cost of 11 million gold marks.

The verdict

Would I rebook? Yes, but only to visit friends who live there. I need to explore more of Germany first.
Return on investment: 6/10
