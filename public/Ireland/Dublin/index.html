Dublin: pubs and live music

Top placements

1. Temple Bar


A busy riverside neighbourhood of cobbled pedestrian lanes, with crowded pubs hosting live folk music and DJ sets. It's promoted as Dublin's "cultural quarter" and the centre of the city's nightlife. Website · Map

2. Guinness Storehouse


Seven floors around a glass atrium shaped like a pint of Guinness. The seventh floor is the Gravity Bar, with views of Dublin and a pint included in the price of admission. In the Perfect Pint bar you can pour your own and even get your selfie printed on top. Website · Map

  • Tip: book ahead. There's also a Connoisseur Bar experience.

3. Jameson Distillery Bow St.


The original site where Jameson Irish Whiskey was distilled until 1971. The Bow Street Distillery was established in 1780, and John Jameson took full ownership in 1805. Website · Map

4. Ha'penny Bridge


A pedestrian bridge over the Liffey, built in May 1816. William Walsh, who ran the ferries across the river, was told to fix them or build a bridge. He built the bridge and was granted the right to charge a ha'penny toll for 100 years. Website · Map

5. The Spire


Also called the Monument of Light: stainless steel, 120 m high and 3 m across at the base. At dusk the top 10 m is lit through 11,884 holes. It has plenty of nicknames, including the stiffy by the Liffey and the spire in the mire. Map

Also seen: the Samuel Beckett Bridge, supported by 31 cables and able to swing open 90 degrees to let ships through. It opened to pedestrians on 10 December 2009.

Good to know

  • The name comes from dubh, "black, dark", and lind, "pool", a dark tidal pool where the River Poddle entered the Liffey, behind Dublin Castle.
  • Arthur Guinness signed a 9,000-year lease on the St. James's Gate site in 1759, at £45 a year.
  • Nelson's Pillar stood where the Spire is now until it was destroyed by a bomb in 1966.
