Rome & Vatican City: 28 centuries, one walkable centre

10 May 2023Italy, Rome / Vatican CityVisited May 2023, Oct 2015

Key takeaways

  • Go for: almost 3,000 years of history packed into a centre you can cover on foot
  • Don't miss: the Pantheon, where the oculus is still open to the sky and rain drains away through the original Roman pipes
  • Best coffee: La Casa del Caffè Tazza d'Oro, the only 5/5 of the trip
  • Skip: the self-guided Segway tour. Riding through Rome's traffic felt dangerous and it was a waste of money
  • Pro tip: pre-book the Vatican and take the first slot of the day, ideally Monday to Wednesday
At a glance
Best for History, food, a long weekend
Time needed 3 days minimum
Budget ££
Best time to go May: great weather. Check for religious events, which make the Vatican extra busy
Getting around Walk across the centre, metro for longer hops

The brief

Two trips, eight years apart. The first was in October 2015 with a partner. I went back in May 2023 for a friend's birthday weekend with a small group of friends.

Top placements

1. The Pantheon


Hadrian's temple to all the gods, and the reason it still stands is that it became a church in 608 AD. Map

  • Tip: look up at the oculus. It has never been covered, so if it's raining, the rain falls straight in.

2. The Colosseum and Palatine Hill


The largest amphitheatre ever built, once holding up to 80,000 people, with the "first nucleus of the Roman Empire" right next door. Map

  • Tip: pair them in one day. Palatine Hill is now a large open-air museum just across from the Colosseum.

3. Trevi Fountain


Rome's biggest Baroque fountain, finished in 1762. Map

  • Tip: throw your coin with your right hand over your left shoulder. Legend says you'll come back.

4. Vatican City


The smallest country in the world, with St. Peter's Basilica, the Sistine Chapel and the Vatican Museums inside it. Map

  • Tip: pre-book and take the first slot of the day, ideally Monday to Wednesday, to cut the wait. Check the calendar for religious events too, as they make it extra busy.

5. Giolitti


Reportedly Rome's oldest ice cream parlour, founded in 1890 and once supplier to the Italian royal family. Map

  • Tip: I went for vanilla and pistachio.

Also worth your time: Piazza Navona, the Spanish Steps, the Arch of Titus, the Victor Emmanuel II Monument viewing platform and Castel Sant'Angelo.

Bars: Drink Kong, Freni e Frizioni (a former car garage), Voodoo Bar and Club Derrière, a speakeasy.

The flat white index

Every city gets a coffee score. Same order everywhere, so cities compare fairly.

Café Score /5 Why
La Casa del Caffè Tazza d'Oro 5 The best cup of the trip
Roscioli Caffè Pasticceria 3 Café and dessert shop
Sant'Eustachio Il Caffè 2 Pouring since 1938 and famous for its lobster tail pastry, but the coffee didn't match the reputation

City score: 4/5. Too often the coffee was dry and bitter. Order what the locals drink and it gets much better.

Ad spotting

What stood out was the lack of ads. Rome seems to keep outdoor advertising away from its historic sites, so the only placements I spotted were in the shopping districts, printed on scaffolding wraps.

Connected

  • Data and paying: nothing of note, which is how it should be
  • Apps that helped: Google Maps and Citymapper

Good to know

  • Around €3,000 is thrown into the Trevi Fountain every day.
  • Vatican City covers 49 hectares and has about 825 residents, making it the smallest state in the world by both measures.
  • Rome is home to several UN agencies, including the Food and Agriculture Organization and the World Food Programme.

The verdict

Would I rebook? Yes. I'm keen to go back once I've explored more of Italy.
Return on investment: 9/10

Next time

  • See: Museo Vespa, the Knights of Malta Keyhole, the Passetto di Borgo (summer tours only), the Borghese Gallery (closed Mondays, book ahead) and a Sunday cycle on the Via Appia Antica, when it's closed to traffic
  • Eat: Antico Forno Roscioli, Trapizzino, Osteria da Fortunata and Testaccio market
