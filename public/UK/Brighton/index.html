Brighton: classic seaside, easy from London

1 July 2015UK, BrightonVisited Jun 2022, Sep 2018, Jul 2018, Jul 2015, Jul 2014

Key takeaways

  • Go for: a classic seaside day out, easy from London
  • Don't miss: The Lanes
  • Best coffee: take your pick, the city scores 5/5 for choice
  • Skip: the British Airways i360, pricey for a short ride
  • Pro tip: pack for pebbles. It's a pebble beach, so bring something to sit on

The brief

Five visits between 2014 and 2022, with a mix of friends and partners.

Top placements

1. The Lanes


A maze of twisting alleyways full of antiques, jewellery, boutique fashion and design shops. They were the centre of the old fishing village of Brighthelmstone and were fully laid out by 1792. Map

2. Brighton Palace Pier


The 1,722 ft Victorian pier, opened in 1899, with fairground rides, bars and deckchairs facing the sea. Map

  • Tip: come back after dark, when it's lit by 67,000 bulbs.

3. Royal Pavilion


George IV's seaside palace, built from 1787, with the domes and minarets John Nash added from 1815. Map

Also seen: the Clock Tower, built in 1888 for Queen Victoria's Golden Jubilee, and the British Airways i360.

Good to know

  • In the 2001 census, 2.6% of Brighton and Hove listed their religion as "Jedi Knight", the highest share in the country.
  • During the First World War the Royal Pavilion became a military hospital, treating over 2,300 men.
  • The i360 was launched as "the world's tallest moving observation tower" until Guinness revoked the record and the Advertising Standards Authority told British Airways to stop using the claim.
