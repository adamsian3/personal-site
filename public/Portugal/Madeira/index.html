Madeira: gardens and views

1 August 2019Portugal, MadeiraVisited Aug 2019

Key takeaways

  • Go for: gardens and views
  • Don't miss: the Monte Sledges
  • Best coffee: Greenhouse Coffee Roaster, 4/5
  • Skip: North Mills Distillery. Not much to see
  • Pro tip: cable car up to Monte, sledge down

A holiday with my partner in August 2019.

Top placements

1. Monte Sledges


One of Madeira's most famous attractions. The sledges are made of wicker and wood, with two or three seats, and are powered by two carreiros (sledge drivers in white uniforms and straw hats) who use their boots as brakes. The ride from Monte to Funchal covers 2 km in about 10 minutes. Website · Map

  • Tip: take the Funchal-Monte cable car up, then the sledge down.

2. Tropical Garden Monte Palace


A big, multi-level garden with exotic trees and flowers, ponds, African sculptures and a mineral museum, over 70,000 m². It can be reached from Funchal by the Funchal-Monte cable car. Website · Map

3. Madeira Botanical Garden


Opened to the public in 1960, on an estate that belonged to the family of William Reid, founder of Reid's Hotel. It includes Louro Bird Park, with around 300 exotic birds. Website · Map

4. Miradouro do Ninho da Manta


An observation deck. Beautiful place and amazing view. Map

  • Tip: take warm jackets, because it blows like hell, but it's worth it.

5. Belmond Reid's Palace

Great afternoon tea spot. Winston Churchill came to the hotel in 1950 to write his memoirs. Website · Map

Also seen:

  • Mercado dos Lavradores, the farmers' market, built in 1940, with produce, flowers and seafood
  • Câmara de Lobos, believed to be João Gonçalves Zarco's original landing point, and where Winston Churchill used to go to paint
  • Santana, a village on the north coast known for its traditional stone houses with steep, triangular thatched roofs
  • The Art of Open Doors (Arte de Portas Abertas), painted doors on Rua Santa Maria
  • Blandy's Wine Lodge, run by the only founding family of the Madeira wine trade still managing its own company, a tradition going back to 1811
  • Seixal Natural Pools, a swimming basin off the sea
  • North Mills Distillery, one of three old mills still operating in Madeira, built in 1927

Good to know

  • Madeira was claimed by Portuguese sailors in the service of Prince Henry the Navigator in 1419 and settled after 1420.
  • There are over 2,170 km of levadas, aqueducts first built in the 16th century to carry water to the south. They provide a network of walking paths.
  • From the 15th to the 19th century the island was the most famous sugar producer in the world. Europeans knew it as "White Gold".
