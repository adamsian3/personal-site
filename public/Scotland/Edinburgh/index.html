Edinburgh: the castle and the views

1 February 2017Scotland, EdinburghVisited Sep 2021, Oct 2016

Key takeaways

  • Go for: the castle and the views
  • Don't miss: Edinburgh Castle
  • Best coffee: Wellington Coffee, 5/5
  • Skip: Camera Obscura & World of Illusions. It's aimed at kids
  • Pro tip: I feel the energy would be higher during the Fringe
At a glance
Best for The castle and the views
Time needed 3 days
Budget ££
Best time to go August, for the Fringe
Getting around Train from London, then walking

The brief

Two trips with my partner, in October 2016 and September 2021.

Top placements

1. Edinburgh Castle

Scotland's most visited paid tourist attraction, with over 2.2 million visitors in 2019. It stands on Castle Rock, the plug of an extinct volcano, which has been occupied since at least the Iron Age. Website · Map

2. Calton Hill


Beyond the east end of Princes Street and part of the city's UNESCO World Heritage Site. A lot to see here: the National Monument, the Nelson Monument, the Dugald Stewart Monument, the old Royal High School, the Robert Burns Monument, the Political Martyrs' Monument and the City Observatory. Map

3. Victoria Street (West Bow)


Its gentle curve and colourful shopfronts make it a favourite spot for photos, postcards and TV adverts. It was built between 1829 and 1834 to improve access around the Old Town. Map

4. Dean Village


A former village just northwest of the city centre, once the "Water of Leith Village", and a grain milling area for more than 800 years. At one time there were eleven working mills. Map

5. The Black Cat


A bar specialising in Scottish whisky, with over 130 malts, and staff well trained for any of them. The Financial Times Magazine called it "one of Edinburgh's top five places to hang out". Website · Map

Also seen:

  • The White Hart Inn, a Grassmarket inn from the early 16th century, a few hundred steps from where public hangings were held. Robert Burns and William Wordsworth were among its visitors
  • The Caley Picture House, the fanciest Spoons. It opened as a cinema in 1923, and Queen and AC/DC are among its past performers
  • Greyfriars Bobby Statue, a life-size statue of a 19th-century Skye terrier said to have sat by his owner's grave for 14 years
  • Camera Obscura & World of Illusions, in the Outlook Tower near the castle

Good to know

  • The Fringe is the world's largest annual international arts festival.
  • Since 2009 the castle has appeared on £10 notes issued by the Clydesdale Bank.
  • The earliest known human habitation in the Edinburgh area was at Cramond, a Mesolithic camp site dated to around 8500 BC.

The verdict

Would I rebook? Yes, for the Fringe.
Return on investment: 8/10
