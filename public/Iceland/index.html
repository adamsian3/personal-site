Iceland: New Year in Reykjavík, the smoky bay

30 December 2021Iceland, ReykjavíkVisited Dec 2021

Key takeaways

  • Go for: New Year in Reykjavík, geothermal spas and a compact, walkable capital
  • Don't miss: the Blue Lagoon
  • Best coffee: Reykjavik Roasters, 5/5
  • Skip: nothing, it was all worth it
  • Pro tip: pre-book the Blue Lagoon
At a glance
Best for New Year, spas, a week away
Time needed A week
Budget £££. Drinks in bars are expensive, so locals tend to drink at home before going out
Best time to go New Year, when anyone can buy fireworks and the whole city lights up
Getting around Reykjavík's centre is walkable. The Blue Lagoon is out near the airport

The brief

A week over New Year 2021 with my partner and her family.

Top placements

1. Blue Lagoon (Bláa lónið)


A man-made lagoon fed by water from the Svartsengi geothermal power plant next door. The milky blue comes from silica, which forms the soft white mud bathers rub on their skin. Bathing opened here in 1987. Map

  • Tip: pre-book your slot.

2. Hallgrímskirkja


At 74.5 m, the largest church in Iceland. It took 41 years to build, from 1945 to 1986, and is named after the poet Hallgrímur Pétursson. Map

  • Tip: go up the tower. The viewing point is just behind the clock face.

3. Íslenski barinn


A bar serving Icelandic classics, including hákarl: Greenland shark, fermented and then hung to dry for four to five months. Strong ammonia smell, fishy taste, very much an acquired one. Map

4. The Icelandic Phallological Museum


The world's largest display of penises: over 300 specimens from more than 100 species of mammal, plus 22 from creatures of Icelandic folklore. Map

5. Reykjavík


Iceland's capital and the site of its first permanent settlement, by Ingólfr Arnarson in 874. The name means "smoky bay", after the steam rising from the hot springs. Map

Also seen: Harpa, Reykjavík's glass concert hall on the harbour Map; SKY Restaurant & Bar Map; and Tjörnin, the small lake in the city centre Map.

Good to know

  • Beer was banned in Iceland until 1 March 1989.
  • Iceland has the smallest population of any NATO member and is the only one with no standing army, just a lightly armed coast guard.
  • During the Second World War, Iceland turned down repeated British requests to station troops there, citing its neutrality.
