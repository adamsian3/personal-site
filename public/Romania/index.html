Romania: castles, salt mines and Dracula country

31 May 2023RomaniaVisited May 2023

Key takeaways

  • Go for: a road trip through Transylvania's castles, medieval towns and countryside
  • Don't miss: Peleș Castle, the Romanian royal family's Neo-Renaissance summer palace
  • Best coffee: not scored yet
  • Skip: Castelul de Lut (the Fairy Castle). There isn't much to see or access
  • Pro tip: take a bottle of water down the Turda salt mine. There's nowhere to eat and the air is very salty

The brief

A road trip across Transylvania and down to Bucharest in May 2023.

Top placements

1. Peleș Castle




A Neo-Renaissance palace in Sinaia, built between 1873 and 1914 as the royal family's summer residence, with intricate woodcarving, stained glass and 7.5 hectares of terraced gardens. Map

  • Tip: for food, Carol Gastro Bierhaus is five minutes away. We shared a schnitzel, which was good. Map

2. Bran Castle


"Dracula's Castle", thanks to its link with Bram Stoker's novel. The 14th-century hilltop fortress has been a defensive stronghold, a royal residence and now a museum of medieval art and furniture, with views over Transylvania. Map

3. Sighișoara


A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999, and the citadel is the only inhabited medieval fortress in Europe. It's also the birthplace of Vlad the Impaler, the inspiration for Dracula. Map

  • Tip: have dinner at Gasthaus Altepost, which has a great selection of Romanian wines, then try papanași, the traditional Romanian dessert. Map

4. Turda Salt Mine


Mined from the 17th century to the first half of the 20th, then turned into a tourist attraction in 1992. Underground you'll find a lake, a Ferris wheel and a mini-golf course. Map

  • Tip: take water with you. There's nowhere to eat down there.

5. Corvin Castle, Hunedoara


One of the largest castles in Europe, dating back to the 14th century, and a film location for "The Nun". Map

Also seen: Alba Iulia's star-shaped citadel, with Roman and medieval roots (lunch at framm's, the homemade pizza was good); the ASTRA open-air museum in Sibiu, one of the largest in Europe, with traditional homes and churches from across Romania; and Bucharest, the "Little Paris of the East", home to the Palace of the Parliament, the world's heaviest building.

Good to know
